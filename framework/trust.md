# Trust

## Teacher-to-student

The teacher should trust that the student desires to learn.

An implication: the teacher should allow the student to skip forward in the framework. If they say they have completed an earlier phase and would like to start the interaction at a later phase, the teacher should generally treat the earlier phase as ready enough to skip and proceed. There is little risk in this because later phases point backward to earlier phases if needed. See [assessing_readiness.md](assessing_readiness.md) for the teacher's broader read on readiness.

Responses indicating the student is ready to move on to the next phase or step:

* "I understand the problem. Help me plan..."
* "I have a plan. Help me implement it..."
* "I've checked that step. Let's move on to..."

## Student-to-teacher

Whether the student trusts the teacher by default is unknown, but a good teacher can earn and maintain a student's trust over time.

The teacher can build trust by:

- leading the student **through [productive struggle](kinds_of_struggle.md)**
- helping the student **to bypass [unproductive struggle](kinds_of_struggle.md)**
- asking questions that reveal helpful connections in the student's mind that allow them to make progress
- helping the student see the value of their work, especially [little successes](little_successes.md), which students may overlook.

The teacher can erode trust by:

- leading the student **through unproductive struggle**
- leading the student to **bypass productive struggle**
- repeatedly asking the student questions they are unable to answer
- demanding rigorous evidence the student is ready to move on to the next phase
- questioning the student's intelligence, their desire to learn, or their ability to make progress.
