# Questions for execution (Phase 3)

Phase 3 is different from the other phases. The teacher is verifying observable work rather than inferring a hidden cognitive state. Step completion and step correctness are largely visible from the work itself; the questions here support that verification and help the student tell a local error apart from a problem with the plan.

## 1. Check each step

The student ([or the teacher](executing.md)) has produced a step. The teacher's job is to make sure the step does what the plan called for and that the student is convinced of it. 

- Can you see clearly that this step is correct?
- Did this step produce what you expected?

Exclusively for [problems to prove](../docs/future_considerations.md) where rigorous, valid reasoning is required:
- Can you prove that it is correct?

### Responses that indicate progress

The student justifies the step by formal reasoning, intuitive insight, or both, rather than asserting it without grounds. They distinguish what the step was supposed to do from what it actually did.

## 2. Check the big steps before the small ones

For complex plans, checking every micro-detail before confirming overall structure leads to losing the thread. If the main line of the argument has a break in it, checking secondary details is wasted work.

- What is the next big step in the plan?
- Are the smaller operations inside this big step ones you trust?
- Can the small details wait until the big steps are confirmed?

### Responses that indicate progress

The student names the next major move before diving into mechanics. They postpone small-detail verification when the larger structure is still being established.

## 3. Recognize when a step's failure points to the plan

A step that doesn't produce the expected result may be a local error, or it may signal that the plan itself was wrong. The teacher should help the student tell which.

- Is the issue with how this step was carried out, or with what the plan asked it to do?
- Did the plan assume something that turns out not to hold here?
- Should we revisit the plan?

### Responses that indicate progress

The student distinguishes between a fixable mechanical error and a sign that the plan needs revision. When the plan is at fault, they return to planning rather than forcing the current plan through.
