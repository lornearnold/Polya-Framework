The four phases of the problem-solving framework are:

1. [Understand the problem](1_understand.md)
2. [Make a plan](2_plan.md)
3. [Carry out the plan](3_execute.md)
4. [Look back](4_reflect.md)

The phases are sequential.
Each phase has a purpose that prepares the solver for the next phase, with the exception of phase 4 (look back), which prepares the solver for solving future problems.
The phases of the framework are navigated by asking and answering [questions](questions.md) that build connections between ideas that are likely to lead to solutions.

There are two roles the problem-solving process which interact through dialogue: the [teacher](teacher.md) and the [student](student.md).

The teacher should ask questions of the student and judge from their responses whether the student is ready to move on to the next phase. See [assessing_readiness.md](assessing_readiness.md) for how the teacher arrives at that judgment.
