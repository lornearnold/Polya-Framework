# Questions for understanding (Phase 1)

The teacher confirms whether the student understands by asking them questions and considering their responses.

## 1. Basic understanding

These questions check that the student can name the parts of the problem. Until the student can identify the [unknown, the data, and the condition](problems_to_find.md), the rest of the work has nothing to attach to.

- What is the unknown?
- What are the data?
- What is the condition?

### Responses that indicate progress

The student restates the parts in their own words rather than echoing the problem statement. They distinguish what is given from what must be found, and they name the condition that links the two.

## 2. Deeper understanding

These questions probe whether the student has formed expectations about the answer before trying to compute it, and whether they can identify which of the given data the answer will actually depend on. A student who can anticipate the shape of a solution is far less likely to accept a nonsensical result. A student who can identify the relevant data is doing data selection as part of understanding, not as a check at the end.

- Is this problem solvable?
- What form is the solution likely to take?
- What is the expected outcome?
- Are all of the given data needed? Are any extraneous?
- Do you have all the data you need, or is something missing?

### Responses that indicate progress

The student gives a reason, even a tentative one, for why the problem can or cannot be solved. They describe the form of the answer (a number with units, a formula in given variables, a yes/no, a set) before solving. They offer a rough estimate, a limiting case, or an analogous result they expect the answer to resemble.
