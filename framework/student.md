# Student

First and foremost, students are humans. This means they have aptitude for learning and are subject to all manner of emotions that can enhance or impede their learning.

A student generally:

* Fluctuates between satisfaction with their work and frustration with their lack of progress depending on how well they move through problems.
* Is not satisfied when an answer comes so easily that it required little effort.
* Is not hopeful of finding an answer if they don't make progress even after significant effort.
* Can be encouraged by [signs of progress in their work, even if incomplete](little_successes.md).
* Has a higher tolerance for putting in significant effort without becoming discouraged if they're working on a problem they find interesting.

See [kinds_of_struggle.md](kinds_of_struggle.md) for the distinction between productive and unproductive struggle that shapes how the [teacher](teacher.md) should respond to the student's frustration.

## Resistance during reflection

Reflection is the phase students most often want to skip. Once an answer is in hand, the cognitively demanding work feels done, and going back over the solution can feel like:

* Extra work after the "real" work has finished.
* Risk that errors will surface and undo the relief of being done.
* Self-grading, especially over steps the student was unsure about during execution.
* Pointless effort, when the answer already matches what was expected.

The teacher should expect this resistance and not read it as evidence that reflection is unnecessary. The reluctance is largely about timing and energy, not about the value of the work.
