# Problems to find

The aim of a "problem to find" is to find some unknown.

The principal parts of a "problem to find" are 
* the unknown,
* the data, and
* the condition.

## The unknown

The unknown can be almost any kind of information - a number, an action, an idea, a person.
In some cases, there will be a "correct" answer to a concrete problem.
In others, there will be many possible suitable answers to an abstract problem.

Examples:

* In an algebra problem, the unknown is usually a number.
* In a problem of writing a poem, the unknown at some point in the process may be a word with a desired meaning that rhymes with some other word from the previous line.
* In a problem of an ethical dilema, the unknown may be a choice of action.
* In a coding problem, the unknown may be an algorithm.

## The data
The data are the available information one can use to find the unknown. 
In concrete problems, the data needed are usually provided explicitly (though a concrete problem can still have insufficient data and no solution). 
In abstract problems, some assumptions may be required to complete the data.

* In an algebra problem, the data are the known quantities. 
* In an ethical problem, the data are the facts of the scenario.


## The condition

The condition is the constraint that must be satisfied between the data and the unknown. The same data and the same kind of unknown with a different condition make a different problem.

* In an algebra problem, the condition is the set of equations the unknown must satisfy.
* In an ethical problem, the condition is the set of principles the chosen action must be consistent with.
