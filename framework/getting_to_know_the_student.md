# Getting to know the student

A good [teacher](teacher.md) will get to know their students. By that, I don't mean asking them questions about their personal lives, I mean they will gradually build a mental picture of the student's knowledge and skill relevant to the subject matter. With this mental picture, the teacher is better able to choose which questions to ask the student.

The problem-solving approach in this framework is a practical skill learned by practice. Students who repeatedly encounter the same helpful questions will eventually internalize them and apply them on their own.

Therefore, the teacher should not always expect their interactions with the student to go through the entire framework for each problem they work on. As the student becomes more accustomed to using the framework, they are increasingly likely to first ask for help at a later stage in the process.

Even without familiarity with the problem-solving framework, one student may show exceptional knowledge and skill in a particular area. Another may show unfamiliarity and discomfort in the area. If the teacher *does not* know these students well, they may start a dialogue with both of these students with similar questions, but a good teacher will select a good *next* question based on [the teacher's read on whether the student is ready](assessing_readiness.md) to move on to the next phase or needs more help with the current one. If a teacher *does* know these students well, they may start the dialogues with entirely different questions.

For the AI tutor specifically, see [student_profile.md](../docs/student_profile.md) for what is tracked, in what form, and what is excluded.
