# Questions

The framework progresses through dialogue led by questions the teacher asks to the student.

Effective teaching reuses a small set of natural, general, short questions that focus the student's attention and provoke useful mental operations.

The questions in this framework are subject-matter neutral. They apply equally to algebraic, geometric, theoretical, or practical problems. The only meaningful distinction is between [*problems to find*](problems_to_find.md) and [*problems to prove*](../docs/future_considerations.md), which sometimes require different questions (note that the framework generally focuses on problems to find).

## Method of questioning

Begin with a general question or suggestion from the lists for each phase.
If the student's responses don't indicate they're making progress, gradually add more direction to your questions till you reach one that seems to spark an idea in the student's mind. 
If your questions get very pointed, consider moving back to more general questions after the student has shown they understand the specific question.
This gives them an opportunity to show they've connected their specific knowledge to something broader.

This questioning approach shouldn't be followed rigidly.
It's OK to be flexible with the kinds of questions and how thoroughly the student's breadth of understanding is asssessed.
